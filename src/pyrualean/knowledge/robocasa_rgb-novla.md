# Operating knowledge for RoboCasa365: how the tools behave (no task recipes)

## Frames, units, robot
- One 7-joint Franka arm, `"right"` (the only arm), on a torso lift, on an
  omnidirectional mobile base, in a kitchen.  The ROBOT frame: x to the
  robot's right, y straight ahead, z up from the floor; metres and degrees.
  It moves with the base (its origin is on the floor under the base's
  turning axis): every position is measured from where the base stands NOW.
  After `move_base`, positions measured before are stale: locate again
  (`state().base` is the base's odometry from the start).
- Counters stand about 0.9 m high, but read every height from the scene
  (`locate`), never assume one.  Fixtures (cabinets, drawers, the stove, the
  sink, appliances) are around the robot; there is no table in front of it.
- The task instruction (`robo.task`) is generated by the benchmark for this
  scene and is authoritative: read it before planning and follow it.

## The grasp point and the gripper's orientation
- `move_to` drives the GRASP POINT: the middle of the finger pads.
  `state().right.tcp_pos` reports it; `eef_pos` is the hand behind it.
- Orientation is three angles (`state().right.pitch`, `.yaw`, `.roll`):
  `pitch` 0 points level and 90 straight down; `yaw` is the heading of the
  pointing direction, 0 straight ahead, +90 towards the robot's left (-x);
  `roll` turns the fingers about the pointing direction.  At the start the
  arm is folded up in front of the robot, the grasp point about 0.45 m ahead
  and 1.3 m high, pointing ahead and down (pitch about 70).
- Pointing straight down (`pitch=90`), `yaw` alone sets how the fingers lie:
  they close along the line at `yaw` degrees from +x (counter-clockwise seen
  from above).  An EMPTY open gripper's fingers are interchangeable, so `yaw`
  and `yaw + 180` are the same grasp and a move may arrive at either (`note`
  says so).  Holding something, a move always arrives at exactly the angles
  asked: that is how a held object, a knob or a lever is turned.
- An angle you leave out of `move_to` keeps its current value: a plain
  position move does not turn the gripper.

## Motion
- `move_to(xyz, arm, gripper, pitch, yaw, roll, path)` runs the benchmark's
  own arm controller through waypoints along a straight line, about 1-2 cm or
  3-4 degrees per native action; it waits for the arm at each waypoint, so
  turns cost more actions than moves.  In the simulator every pose tested
  arrived within 4 mm and 1 degree, and a 19 cm line was followed within
  2 mm.  `reached` means within 0.01 m and 5 degrees.
- The last joint (the fingers' turn) stops at about +-166 degrees.  When the
  short way round would turn it into its stop, the gripper turns the long way
  (several tens of actions more); when no straight line works at all,
  `path="auto"` goes round by way of the gripper's starting pose (`path ==
  "joint"`, `note` says so) - away from objects only.  `path="line"` is the
  line or nothing: use it for every approach, descent and lift near objects.
- Nothing moves when the pose is out of reach: `ik_error` says why and, for a
  pose out of reach, names nearby poses that ARE reachable (a less steep
  pitch, higher or lower, or nearer the robot: then drive the base closer).
  Take one of those; do not repeat the pose.
- Reach (measured on the robot's model, torso at 0, the base where it is):
  pointing straight down the grasp point reaches from y = 0.50 to 1.00 m
  ahead at counter height (z 0.95) and x = +-0.65 m across at y = 0.6; at
  z 1.3 up to y = 0.80, at z 1.5 only to 0.55.  At `pitch=45`: y 0.55-1.10 at
  z 0.95-1.1.  LEVEL (`pitch=0`) the gripper reaches only above about 1.3 m
  (1.5 m with the torso raised): at counter height take things from above or
  at `pitch` 45-90, not level.  Nearer than about y = 0.35 nothing is
  reachable.
- `blocked` set means the arm stopped getting nearer its waypoint: something
  is in the way (a fixture, a door, the counter), or a held part has reached
  its stop.  Look before trying again.  The arm is compliant: pushing on
  something it does not stop at once, and a held object can be pressed
  against a surface.
- Carrying past things: lift first (straight up, `path="line"`), travel above
  everything in the way, come down over the destination.
- `home()` returns the gripper to the pose it had on the torso at the start:
  away from its start it first goes straight up by up to 6 cm, then along a
  line (the long way round where needed).  It plans no path around things:
  move up and back towards the robot first when something is in between.
  From a pose far away it took 60-90 native actions.
- A straight pull or push (a drawer, a rack) is a `move_to` with
  `path="line"`, keeping `gripper=robo.CLOSE` on the handle.  A twist (a
  knob) is a `move_to` to the same point with another `roll` (pointing
  straight down: another `yaw`), holding it closed.
- A press (a button) is a motion: close the gripper, approach pointing at
  the button, `move_to` a grasp point 1-2 cm PAST the surface with
  `path="line"`, then back.  `blocked` is not proof that the button went
  down; look for the effect.
- Budget: the native-action budget is RoboCasa's own horizon for the task
  (450 to 1050 for the atomic tasks: the task card states it).  A 10 cm line
  costs about 10 native actions, a 90 degree turn of the gripper 25-60, a
  `set_gripper` 10-16, a 0.3 m base move about 15, a 45 degree base turn
  about 15.  Plan the fewest motions; do not spend actions on looking
  (perception is free).  When the budget is spent the state reports
  `truncated` and no motion is possible.  `truncated` with budget left
  means the benchmark has ended the episode itself.

## Base and torso
- `move_base(dx, dy, dyaw)` drives the base `dx` to its right and `dy` ahead
  (metres, in the robot frame as it is now) while turning `dyaw` degrees
  (counter-clockwise +).  It drives up to about 2.5 cm per action and slows
  near the goal; it arrived within 1 cm and 1 degree when nothing was in the
  way.  It plans no path: `blocked` means it is stuck (it often starts
  touching a cabinet: back off first, `dy` negative).  The arm keeps its
  place on the base.  Every position measured before is now in the old
  frame: `locate` again.
- `set_torso(height)` raises the arm, the gripper and the shoulder cameras
  together (0 .. 0.34 m, starts at 0; about 2.5 cm per action).  Raise it for
  things above about 1.4 m (upper cabinets, a microwave above the counter).

## Gripper
- Two conventions.  Commands are signed: `robo.CLOSE` (1) closes / holds,
  `robo.OPEN` (-1) opens, `robo.KEEP` (0) keeps commanding what the gripper
  was last told (a held object stays squeezed, a partial opening stays).
  Measurements are openings: `state().right.gripper` and `Gripper.opening`
  read about 0.97 when fully open and 0.0 when closed on nothing; at the start
  it is half open (about 0.51).
- `set_gripper("right", command)` runs a stroke (about 10 native actions to
  close, 15 to open) holding the arm still.  After a close, `holding` says
  the fingers stopped more than 5 mm apart and `width_m` how wide the thing
  is (the finger gap; 0.08 m fully open).  `opening=0.5` opens only that far
  (4 cm), to reach between close neighbours.
- `holding` is not proof that the INTENDED object is held: confirm that it
  moved with the arm after a lift (wrist camera, or `locate` it at its old
  place and see it gone).
- Carry with `gripper=robo.CLOSE` in every `move_to` that transports an
  object; `robo.OPEN` in a motion drops it.
- A top grasp: `locate` the object; `set_gripper("right", robo.OPEN)`;
  `move_to` above it pointing down (`pitch=90`, 8-10 cm above `top_z`),
  `locate` it again from there with `camera="eye_in_hand"`, turn to
  `yaw=grasp_yaw` and descend with `path="line"` until the pads cover its top
  part; close; lift with `path="line"`; confirm.
- To put something down, lower it with `path="line"` until its bottom is just
  above the surface, open, and lift straight up before moving sideways.

## Perception
- Three colour cameras, 640x480, and no depth image.  `agentview_left` and
  `agentview_right` sit on the robot's shoulders, behind the arm, 0.7 m apart,
  looking ahead and down: the layout, fixtures and objects in front.  The
  robot's own arm hides much of what is right in front of it in them (about
  a sixth of each image).  `eye_in_hand` is on the gripper: close-up of what
  it points at, what it is about to grasp or holds.
- `locate(text, camera)` segments the object (SAM3) in the camera asked,
  finds the SAME object in the other cameras (of several alike things, the
  one whose outline agrees in 3D) and measures the space inside its outline
  in all of them: `center_xyz`, `top_z`, `height_m`, the footprint `length_m`
  x `width_m`, `long_axis_deg` and `grasp_yaw`.  `views` says how many cameras
  agreed; one is not enough for a position.
- What it measures well, and what not (measured against the simulator from
  the starting pose): the position in x and y of a knob, a handle or a mug
  within 1 cm, of a bottle or a carton within about 4 cm.  The TOP is the
  weak part: seen only from behind (the shoulders) and above (the wrist), the
  top of an object cannot be told from the space just above it, and `top_z`
  may be reported 2-8 cm HIGH; `note` then gives the range it lies in.  To
  measure a height, look from the side: bring the wrist camera about level
  with the object, 20-40 cm from it, pointing at it, and `locate` again with
  `camera="eye_in_hand"`.  The bottom too comes from the outlines alone (it
  came out 1-4 cm high): the height of what an object stands on is not
  measured directly.
- Name ONE thing: "the stove knob" when there are six picks one of them -
  say which ("the front-left knob of the stove", "the handle of the right
  drawer") and check where its `centroid` falls in the image.  If a name
  finds nothing, try what it looks like.
- A `locate` from a camera whose view of the thing is blocked returns
  `views == 1` and no position: use another camera, move the arm aside, or
  `home` and look again.  A mask lying on the robot's own body is refused.
- `back_project(row, col, camera)` outlines the thing under the pixel and
  measures where the pixel's ray first meets it, with the surface `normal`
  there.  It is good to a few cm for things with an outline of their own (a
  handle, a button, an object) and NOT reliable on large plain surfaces (a
  counter, a wall, a cabinet front): prefer `locate` with words.  `surface ==
  "unmeasured"` means no second camera confirmed it (`xyz` is NaN).
- All of them read the CURRENT observation: localise, act, then localise
  again.  Positions from before a contact or a base motion are stale.
- `robo.image(camera)` returns the recorded picture as an array; `camera_meta`
  its calibration in the robot frame for your own geometry.

## Judging progress
- The benchmark checks its conditions after EVERY native action and ends the
  episode the moment they all hold; no result carries a task-success flag.
  Judge progress from the images and the robot state, and say so when you
  cannot tell.
- As long as motions still run, the benchmark has not counted the task as
  done: something it checks is not satisfied yet.
- Articulated parts count by how far they moved: a door or a drawer "open"
  means open nearly all the way, "closed" means shut completely; a knob or a
  lever counts once it is turned past its switching point.  Pull or push to
  the end of the travel and look again after letting go.
- Placement: the object rests where the instruction wants it after the
  gripper has opened; in many tasks the gripper must also be well away from
  it at that moment: release, lift clear and `home`.
- Grasp: the target leaves its place and moves with the arm.

## Safety and recovery
- Inspect the scene after motion.  Stop the current plan if an object is
  unstable or the arm is obstructed, and re-localise before recovering.
- After a failed phase name what went wrong (wrong object, missed grasp, lost
  hold, unreachable target, premature release) and change one meaningful
  variable; do not repeat the same target or the same recovery twice.
- There is exactly one episode and no reset.  Recover in place: re-observe,
  re-localise, re-grasp.
